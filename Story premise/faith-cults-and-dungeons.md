# Echoes of Alderfall: Faith, Cults, Camps, and Dungeons

Status: Target fiction and implementation specification. These locations, factions, encounters, and outcomes are proposed content, not implemented gameplay.

Implementation exception, 22 September 2026: the existing southern main quests now include an inspectable altered welcome, a blocked guest-vessel outlet, and an ordered action to disconnect Ember before releasing one captive fire guest. See [the main-story contracts](../Java/docs/main-story-rewrite-pass.md) and [runtime transcript](../Java/docs/main-story-dialogue.md). This uses existing regional quest sites and does not yet implement the complete temple layout, faction encounters, or alternative outcomes proposed below.

Read with the [Setting and Story Bible](world-framework.md) and [Narrative Rewrite Brief](narrative-rewrite-brief.md). All new religious institutions, rites, and supernatural rules below are original inventions for this fantasy setting. Their regional inspirations are described in the bible.

The [Regional Bestiary and Bosses](regional-bestiary-and-bosses.md) develops the world's beasts and independent powers alongside these institutions. Regional adventures include natural and supernatural predators, jötunn-inspired giants, serpents, and guardians as well as human factions; cults are one source of conflict among several.

## 1. Faith as part of life

People practice faith to give thanks, mourn, celebrate, seek guidance, maintain relationships, and find company as well as to obtain protection. A temple should have meals, work, disagreements, laughter, and visitors. Its significance cannot depend entirely on a secret chamber full of enemies.

Distinguish five things when writing a religious encounter:

| Layer | Question | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Experience | What actually happened? | A lamp burned after its oil ran out. |
| Interpretation | What does someone believe that means? | A keeper believes Avar accepted a prayer. |
| Teaching | What principle is drawn from the belief? | Light should be offered to travelers. |
| Institution | Who maintains and administers the practice? | A sanctuary provides oil, attendants, and guest rooms. |
| Abuse or dispute | What particular action is contested? | A prior refuses light and shelter to people who will not submit to inspection. |

Showing a real magical effect does not automatically prove every theological claim made about it. Characters can share a practice and disagree about its meaning. An unbeliever can repair a temple roof, while a devout person can oppose its priest.

Use precise faction names in narration. An NPC may call a rival a cult, but the author must know whether it is a voluntary mystery society, an isolated local tradition, or an organization using coercion and violence. Hostility comes from actions and circumstances, not the word attached to a belief.

## 2. Temples and regional traditions

### North: Hearthhouses of Hedra

**Belief:** Hedra of the Last Hearth watches over those who keep shelter available when winter makes survival uncertain. Communities dispute whether protection should extend to a pursuing enemy and how to share stores during scarcity.

**People:** a hearthkeeper, cooks, travelers, local families, memorial carvers, and an elected store steward. Some hearthkeepers also serve as priests; others regard maintaining the house as sufficient service.

**Place:** the proposed **Last Fire Hall**, near Highwall's outer road. Its public hall surrounds a long hearth. A pantry, drying room, sleeping loft, rescue shed, and memorial yard explain how it functions. An older burial passage beneath the yard connects it to the Wake Road.

**Practices:** light a household taper from the hall before winter; leave a place for a missing traveler at a memorial meal; carry spare mittens to a rescue gathering. These acts show care even when no magic occurs.

**Encounter rule:** within this hall's old boundary, lowering a weapon and being formally received grants a guest protection until they attack or voluntarily depart. The rule applies to individual guests, including an enemy deserter. It does not make every northern hearth a combat-free zone.

**Dispute:** a store steward wants to turn away a raider who knows where missing children were taken. Cassia can defend shelter without asking survivors to forgive him. Vesper wants room for families evacuated from the grove.

**Ordinary scene:** a child accuses a gruff rescuer of taking two ceremonial cakes. He explains that one is for his absent partner, then admits he ate both last year.

### South: Courts of the Open Sun

**Belief:** Avar's light reveals and sustains; worshippers disagree about when truth should be made public and what privacy compassion requires. The Unbanked Flame are independent beings, not automatically Avar's servants.

**People:** lamp tenders, singers, engineers, guesthouse stewards, teachers, and priests. The water council retains authority over public channels; a sanctuary cannot simply claim every well.

**Place:** the proposed **Court of the First Cup** in Sanctum. A shaded guest court leads to a teaching hall, public fountain, lamp gallery, workshop, and restricted vessel chamber. The oldest foundation descends toward the Sunken Shrine.

**Practices:** offer water before questioning a traveler; sing thanks at the opening of repaired channels; hold a voluntary dawn testimony before witnesses; allow private mourning behind screened alcoves.

**Encounter rule:** the shrine's paired cooling basins regulate a vessel containing a fire-being. Steady, separated flames indicate a functioning vent. Flames bending toward a person indicate extraction. This is a local mechanism the player can inspect and test, not a universal law of southern magic.

**Dispute:** keepers want the ward to endure; engineers want failing components replaced; Samir discovers that the vessel's occupant cannot withdraw from its service. Several faithful keepers help him challenge the binding.

**Ordinary scene:** a lamp tender rehearses a difficult hymn while an engineer taps the correct rhythm on a pipe. They disagree about the tune and agree that the water must flow before sunset.

### West: Sanctuaries of the Open Bough

**Belief:** the **Wayfarer Beneath the Bough**, an original western holy figure, is remembered as a host who welcomed strangers without owning them. Some identify the figure with a saintly traveler; others with a woodland power. The sanctuaries negotiate with the Briar Courts but do not worship every court ruler.

**People:** hosts, ferrymen, gardeners, sworn escorts, musicians, and sanctuary advocates. Local nobles fund some houses and resent their protection of debtors in others.

**Place:** the proposed **Briarbridge Guest Abbey**. Its bridge chapel, orchard, travelers' rooms, petition bench, kitchen, and feast hall are connected by a public path. A sealed mirror-door beneath the orchard formerly admitted otherworldly guests.

**Practices:** declare whether a gift is free; plant a tree to mark a completed journey; exchange stories at an autumn supper; allow someone under pursuit to ask for a hearing.

**Encounter rule:** in the old feast hall, receiving food after accepting a named host's invitation establishes a particular guest relationship. Visitors can ask the terms, refuse the food, or remain under the abbey's separate protection. Eating ordinary food elsewhere has no hidden penalty.

**Dispute:** a river baron wants the abbey to surrender a fugitive. A Briar envoy claims that the same person is a missing retainer. Seraphine can distinguish a genuine invitation from a transfer of service; Aria doubts whether either claimant has asked the fugitive what they want.

**Ordinary scene:** a novice worries that an orchard guest is a disguised court noble. The gardener is more concerned that the guest keeps pocketing unripe pears.

### Center: Seedhouses and Hearth Chapels

**Belief:** the Keeper of the Returning Seed is honored through renewal, seasonal work, and care for what survives a household. Different communities disagree about whether the Keeper is one power or a role shared by many.

**People:** gardeners, seed keepers, household attendants, celebrants, craftspeople, and burial custodians. Village expertise often sits beside formal religious learning.

**Place:** the proposed **House of Returning Seed** near Oakhaven. It contains a communal oven, seed library, teaching garden, meeting room, and quiet grove. Its old underground root beds are reachable through a maintenance stair.

**Practices:** exchange viable seed after harvest; carry the first bread to an ill neighbor; celebrate a new household with a shared meal; leave winter pruning until the local grove's rest has ended.

**Encounter rule:** this seedhouse's Hearthkin responds to tending and ordinary use, not to valuable offerings. The player learns this by watching it ignore a donated jewel and help an apprentice repair the oven.

**Dispute:** a steward wants to accept a noble's donation that would privatize the garden. The keeper opposes the terms but still needs funds for its damaged roof. Maera can investigate historical ownership; Vesper can show why moving the root beds endangers their inhabitants.

**Ordinary scene:** a harvest play gives the Keeper an enormous painted nose. The oldest attendant insists that the joke was better in her childhood and volunteers to play the part.

### Fenlands: Houses of the Listening Bell

**Belief:** the living should answer calls for help without compelling the dead to remain on watch. Bellkeepers communicate with the Deep Listeners; they disagree about how much the answering sounds reveal of those beings' intentions.

**People:** navigators, bellwrights, healers, salvage crews, funeral singers, and families who maintain small local bells. Expertise travels between villages rather than belonging to one great temple.

**Place:** the proposed **Reedwake Bellhouse** at Mireford. A raised clinic adjoins a bell tower, boat shed, rope-drying loft, and flood memorial. Underwater passages link the older foundation to Miredepth.

**Practices:** ring different calls for help, safe passage, and remembrance; hold boat festivals when seasonal channels reopen; repair a dead person's work bell before retiring it.

**Encounter rule:** the local three-note rescue call opens a temporary route through the submerged foundation. Repetition without an answering interval confuses it with a summons that keeps the dead attending. Teach the difference through a safe demonstration before the dungeon.

**Dispute:** grieving families want to hear lost relatives again. Lyra wants a contaminated rope shipment isolated. Ysra refuses to allow a public memorial to become an involuntary summons.

**Ordinary scene:** the bellwright can identify which apprentice tied a knot by its shape, but cannot remember where she put her own spectacles.

### East: Sanctuaries of the Returning Tide

**Belief:** coastal sanctuaries honor the Keeper of the Returning Tide and maintain particular relationships with crossings, the missing, and remembered objects. Inland traditions follow different powers and customs.

**People:** crossing keepers, boat builders, performers, fishermen, attendants, and families bringing objects for remembrance or release. Provincial authorities negotiate access; they do not own every crossing.

**Place:** the proposed **Sanctuary of the Ninth Landing**. A public landing leads through a washing court to a covered assembly space, object storehouse, repair workshop, and lantern pier. At a particular tide, a second pier becomes visible beneath the first.

**Practices:** clean and repair an inherited object before deciding to keep or retire it; carry a light along a new crossing; remember the missing by name without claiming certainty about their fate.

**Encounter rule:** a specific old sail remembers a route absent from modern water. Its repaired pattern and the correct tide reveal that route. Respectful conduct helps people cooperate, but crossing eligibility is not a vague test of spiritual purity.

**Dispute:** a provincial official wants to destroy objects associated with disappearances. Nao distinguishes dangerous bindings from harmless Remembered Things. Calder recognizes workmanship but needs the keeper's knowledge of the route.

**Ordinary scene:** a stage mask interrupts rehearsal to demand the older ending. The performers bargain for one traditional performance in return for its cooperation with the new play.

## 3. Religious factions and hostile cults

Each group needs a sincere appeal, an organization, internal differences, and observable conduct. Members know different amounts. A frightened recruit, a paid guard, a committed leader, and a captive are not interchangeable enemies.

| Faction | Promise and recruitment | Actual practice and disagreement | Relationship to Vaelthara |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ashen Keel** — north | Fallen defenders will return so no village faces winter raiders alone; recruits among bereaved families and abandoned soldiers. | Leaders fasten ancestral names to a war banner. Some recruits believe service is voluntary and will defect when shown otherwise. Armed binders enforce it. | Kharvok supplies binding techniques; local ambition predates his involvement. |
| **Closed Dawn** — south | Permanent light and reliable protection through disciplined service; offers food and warded shelter. | The inner circle extracts vitality through altered lamp seals. The prior calls the losses necessary; some keepers genuinely do not know the cause of the sickness. | Accepts a herald's help while publicly claiming independent religious authority. |
| **Antler's Due** — west | Release from debt and humiliation through service to a glorious hunt. | Leaders mark unwilling people as quarry to gain court favor. Initiates disagree when their promised symbolic chase becomes real killing. | An independent movement courting a Briar noble; no required demonic link. |
| **Still Harvest** — center | No child should hunger or die before their time; attracts families frightened by loss. | A small inner group keeps a grove in arrested growth, using visitors to replace exhausted living anchors. Most supporters know only its apparently miraculous fruit. | Independent; Vaelthara may later offer protection without having founded it. |
| **Unending Vigil** — Fenlands | The drowned can come home and continue protecting their families. | Sustains a summons that traps the dead and contaminates bell-rope. Some members favor a final farewell once they understand the cost. | Velmora exploits the summons; human grief and decisions sustain it locally. |
| **Unbroken Mask** — east | Preserve family knowledge and loved voices forever. | Voluntary remembrance becomes coercive when leaders copy living people into masks and conceal the originals. Artisans and families can oppose this from within the same tradition. | A regional power seeking control of crossings; initially independent. |
| **Merciful Hand** — across regions | Safe roads, relief stores, and an end to supernatural uncertainty under Vaelthara. | Public relief houses coexist with compulsory pledges, confinement of dissenters, and armed enforcement. Some aid workers try to keep helping while rejecting those demands. | Directly serves Vaelthara; each cell adapts to local needs rather than replacing local faith. |

Also include voluntary secret societies with no hostile agenda. The proposed **Society of the Last Lamp** keeps vigil with dying strangers and anonymously pays funeral costs. Its secrecy protects private grief. A suspicious emblem can lead to an act of kindness as well as a dungeon.

## 4. Enemy camps as inhabited places

A camp has a reason to exist, a supply route, an authority structure, a population, and a plan. Its shrine reflects those realities. A raider's small prayer corner may be sincere personal worship even when the raiding itself is criminal.

Design the camp in readable layers:

1. **Approach:** signs of its activities, escape tracks, sentry signals, and a way to assess danger before entering.
2. **Daily life:** cooking, stores, bedrolls, tool repair, animals, and evidence of who lives there.
3. **Authority:** command tent, meeting ring, pay records, punishment place, or relic platform.
4. **Ritual work:** materials, attendants, rehearsal, and an observable effect whose purpose can be investigated.
5. **Dependent or dissenting people:** prisoners, recruits considering departure, hired workers, visitors, or an empty space explaining their absence.
6. **Connection below:** a guarded excavation, flooded stair, burial entry, or transport shaft that explains why the camp occupies this site.

Do not require six separate rooms in a small camp. A cooking fire can host a dissenting conversation; a marked cargo crate can reveal the ritual's supply chain.

### Three camp treatments

**Ashen Keel muster:** an abandoned northern rescue station now supplies Frosthollow's binders. Ancestor tokens hang beside ordinary drying clothes. A veteran teaches recruits to copy names onto banner strips. Cutting every strip at once releases disoriented dead; securing their destination first lets the player separate willing defenders from compelled ones. A physical assault remains possible, with different survivor reactions and a harder banner encounter.

**Closed Dawn caravan enclosure:** a southern camp receives sick pilgrims and transports sealed lamp vessels to the Sunken Shrine. Guards claim quarantine; a locked exit and concealed departure register reveal confinement. A kitchen worker can show the service route without knowing the extraction rite. Recruits can leave when a safe route exists; committed warders defend the vessels.

**Antler's Due hunting lodge:** a western bandit site stages ritual hunts under borrowed court symbols. Fresh food and expensive tack contrast with unpaid stable workers. A novice thinks the next quarry volunteered. Showing the captive's attempt to escape can expose the lie; wearing a stolen mask alone does not persuade everyone that the player belongs.

Goblins in the existing Redcap camp may include leaders, laborers, spiritual practitioners, and coerced followers. Assign the camp's faction through its actual story. A goblin's species does not prove allegiance to Antler's Due or to any cult.

## 5. Dungeon design: the place existed before the fight

Every major dungeon brief must establish its original purpose, who altered it, why it is dangerous now, what still works, and who wants to use it afterward. A ruined temple can contain a functioning kitchen hearth and a failed sacred mechanism; an occupied mine can acquire a shrine without becoming a temple in retrospect.

Teach supernatural rules in three steps: a low-risk demonstration, a situation where the player applies the rule, and a final encounter that combines it with another pressure. Provide two independent clues to a required rule, one of which is available without a specific companion or rare item.

Religious images serve as evidence and navigation. Repeated symbols, damaged inscriptions, reused rooms, smells, sounds, offerings, and tool marks tell the player how practice changed. Not every clue should be a readable diary.

### Regional dungeon roster

| Region and site | Previous life and present occupation | Traversal or encounter rule | Final encounter and aftermath |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| North: **Frosthollow, Hall Beneath the Cairn** | A burial passage and emergency refuge occupied by Ashen Keel binders. | Calling a carved name releases one defender from the banner long enough to cross its post; a practice alcove teaches the response. | Kharvok's reinforcements diminish as name bindings are resolved. Survivors reopen the upper refuge while the lower dead depart or resume chosen duties. |
| South: **Sunken Shrine, Vessel Below the Court** | A guest sanctuary built around a negotiated fire ward, converted into an extraction chamber. | Paired basins reveal and control pressure; safe vents provide both a ritual route and a tactical advantage. | Break the prior's apparatus and deal with its released heat. The ward's future depends on restored water, an evacuation, or a new voluntary agreement. |
| West: **Crowhook, the Borrowed Hunt** | A ferry customs post and smugglers' vault beneath a hunting lodge. | Marked doorways redirect quarry while the hunt horn sounds. The player can learn the marks, interrupt the horn, or take the guarded service route. | Confront the Hunt-Marshal and the court creature he has summoned. Rescue and evidence outcomes determine who can reclaim the crossing. |
| Center: **Stonegate, the Garden of Unspent Years** | Burial terraces linked to an old seed sanctuary; Still Harvest maintains a suspended grove below them. | Roots draw warmth from occupied anchor beds. Disconnecting a bed safely requires rerouting its flow first. | Free living anchors and face a guardian kept alive beyond its natural cycle. The site can return to seasonal growth and remembrance. |
| Fenlands: **Miredepth, the Infirmary Below the Bell** | A flood refuge and clinic whose rescue call never ended. | Bell sequences open passages and change which voices can answer; silencing everything closes routes as well as suppressing enemies. | Resolve Velmora's control and the continuing summons. Rope contamination and ordinary illness still require treatment afterward. |
| East: **House of a Hundred Departures** — new | A performance hall and object sanctuary occupied by the Unbroken Mask. | Masks replay observed actions; unfamiliar players can learn patterns from a safe rehearsal room before facing moving barriers. | Stop forced copying and confront a guardian assembled from stolen roles. Return voluntary memorial masks without treating every Remembered Thing as hostile. |

Blackvault remains a separate campaign dungeon about the old network's accumulated magical waste. Merciful Hand agents salvage it for Vaelthara; Damar can identify containment records and actual damage. It should not become the secret headquarters responsible for every local faction.

The eastern House provides evidence for the Tides crossing chapter but does not grant an additional stone. The Sunken Shrine's vessel is the larger ward apparatus connected to Samir's family reliquary; the two are related objects, not inconsistent names for the same portable item.

## 6. Complete first content slice: The Guest Below the Flame

This proposed slice connects one functioning temple, one hostile camp, one dungeon, one companion chapter, and return conversations. It can be completed without recruiting Samir. Solari and a local engineer provide essential knowledge; Samir adds family stakes and alternative conversations.

### Cast and knowledge

| Person | What they know at first | What they want | What they are mistaken about or concealing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solari | The public ward is flickering and pilgrims have fallen ill. | Keep the guest court safe and investigate before accusing its keepers. | Assumes the prior still follows the authorized rite. |
| Engineer Nima — new | The return channel has been deliberately closed; reopening it needs a replacement coupling. | Restore cooling without destroying the public fountain. | Does not know what inhabits the vessel. |
| Samir | His family hymn addresses a presence as a guest; his reliquary answers the larger shrine. | Hear the presence and understand his mother's actions. | Hopes the family binding was an innocent mistake. |
| Keeper Tarek — new | Pilgrims grow weak after the nightly rite; the register excludes those who asked to leave. | Save his sister, who remains inside the camp. | Hides his work on transport rosters and fears punishment. |
| Prior Edras — new | Extraction sustains the failing ward and the herald can increase it. | Preserve his authority and what he regards as the only available protection. | Conceals the voluntary venting rite and exaggerates the impossibility of alternatives. |
| Vessel guest | Remembers the first agreement and feels the current extraction. | End compulsion and survive release. | Knows little of the present town's dependence on the ward. |

Nima, Tarek, and Edras are local fictional characters, not identities inferred from existing NPC names. Assign new stable IDs during implementation after checking for collisions.

### Connected locations and evidence

1. **Court:** inspect a basin showing inward-pulled flame. Nima demonstrates ordinary outward venting with a small lamp. Solari authorizes inspection; the player can also reach the public areas independently.
2. **Camp:** find a patient token and the locked departure gate. Tarek offers testimony about the rite if he has reason to expect his sister can be protected. A transport roster and an optional rescue provide alternative evidence if he will not speak.
3. **Upper shrine:** restore the cooling return or establish an evacuation route. A relief carving shows two open channels; a maintenance panel explains their operation in plain terms.
4. **Lower shrine:** speak to the guest through the listening grille. It can confirm whether the ward draws from it willingly and describe the original vent. Samir recognizes a line from the family hymn; the grille also carries an accessible translation.
5. **Vessel chamber:** confront Edras and, if he refuses to yield, defeat the herald and its armed defenders. Prepared vents reduce the encounter's heat hazards. Breaking the vessel without preparation creates a distinct containment task, not an unannounced game over.
6. **Return:** resolve the ward's future, treat released patients, and show changes in the fountain, guest rooms, lamp gallery, and surviving characters' routines.

### Encounter jobs

- **Warders** protect control levers and move to block access. They are trained defenders, not priests whose only behavior is a melee attack.
- **Cantors** maintain the extraction's rhythm. Interrupting or convincing one weakens a channel; their behavior changes when patients escape.
- **Vessel attendants** repair damaged apparatus. Some surrender once the guest's account is heard; others continue under the prior's orders.
- **Heat remnants** follow the apparatus's emissions. They are magical hazards given form, not proof that the fire guest is malicious.
- **The herald** exploits unstable pressure during the final encounter. Its danger is readable through a shared visual and audio warning before discharge.

These behaviors require implementation. A first version can use explicit staged encounters and interactions; the existence of a monster sprite does not establish support for any of these roles.

### Resolution rules

| Resolution | Required preparation | Concrete result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Release after evacuation** | A verified exit and a temporary place for displaced guests. | The being leaves; the ward goes dark, residents use ordinary shelter, and repairs begin. |
| **Restore a voluntary ward** | Functional cooling, withdrawal mechanism demonstrated, and the being's agreement. | A weaker ward continues on limited terms; it cannot promise permanent protection. |
| **Stabilize, then return** | Functional cooling and an explicit finite follow-up stage. | Immediate harm stops; liberation remains unresolved. The quest stays open with a clear next action. |
| **Emergency rupture** | Available during confrontation without the other preparations. | The binding breaks violently; player action determines who is rescued and which structures survive. A follow-up handles survivors and shelter. |

An apparently benevolent promise does not establish consent. The being can refuse the voluntary ward; the evacuation route remains available. Ordinary dialogue browsing does not secretly kill patients. Any timed emergency must begin at a clearly announced event and have readable consequences.

## 7. Implementation against the current project

The current game provides staged `SEARCH`, `VISIT`, `TALK`, `CHOICE`, `DEFEAT`, and other quest kinds in `Java/src/main/java/com/alderfall/game/content/Quest.java`. Names alone do not establish full mechanics: for example, `ESCORT` currently participates in inspect-style handling. Confirm behavior before promising a moving escorted NPC, surrender system, dynamic flood, or combat interruption.

`DialogueContext` already carries quest branch outcomes, topic counts, and recent companion memory text. `GameState` records quest outcomes and memories; `SaveSystem` serializes those collections. Reuse these where their semantics fit. Evidence provenance, NPC-specific disclosures, temple access, and persistent site transformations still need explicit design and implementation review.

### Content and state ownership

| Concern | Implementation target |
| --- | --- |
| Quest stages, descriptions, NPCs, enemies, and rewards | `Java/src/main/java/com/alderfall/game/content/GameData.java` and the quest model. |
| Evidence-aware conversations | `Java/src/main/java/com/alderfall/game/dialogues/DialogueLibrary.java`, companion modules, and a context populated from actual state. |
| State changes and guards | `Java/src/main/java/com/alderfall/game/state/GameState.java`; validate actions before applying outcomes. |
| Sacred sites, camps, dungeon layouts, props, and transitions | `Java/src/main/java/com/alderfall/game/map/WorldMap.java`; preserve reachable routes and prop indexing. |
| Save persistence and migration | `Java/src/main/java/com/alderfall/game/systems/SaveSystem.java`; save facts and decisions, not just rendered dialogue text. |
| Verification | Existing diagnostics under `Java/src/main/java/com/alderfall/game/diagnostics/`, plus focused checks for new outcome and evidence rules. |

Existing candidate site IDs include `dungeon_frosthollow_1`, `dungeon_crowhook_outpost_1`, `dungeon_stonegate_1`, `dungeon_miredepth_1`, and `dungeon_blackvault_1`. Inspect how floor IDs and transitions are generated before placing a stage on a particular floor. A label in quest prose is not a map placement.

The Sunken Shrine exists in story text but this review has not established a dedicated playable dungeon for it. Its concrete map, the caravan enclosure, and the eastern House need verified placement or new maps. Do not attach their stages to unrelated graveyard or camp markers just to make the objective appear.

### State model for the first slice

The following are proposed semantic fields, not registered engine keys:

| State | Allowed form and purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `vessel.evidence` | A set of inspected or witnessed facts with source IDs: inward flame, closed return, departure lock, testimony, original vent. |
| `vessel.disclosed_to` | Per-NPC facts actually told or witnessed; player knowledge does not automatically become NPC knowledge. |
| `vessel.cooling` | `blocked`, `repaired`, or `destroyed`. |
| `vessel.exit` | `unverified`, `secured`, or `blocked`; revalidate before an evacuation action. |
| `vessel.guest_terms` | `unknown`, `refused`, or `accepted`; any offer states its current conditions. |
| `vessel.resolution` | `unresolved`, `stabilized_pending`, `released`, `voluntary_ward`, or `ruptured`. |
| `vessel.prior_status` | `in_command`, `yielded`, `escaped`, or `defeated`; independent of the guest's fate. |
| `vessel.survivors` | Explicit status of named people; derive population summaries from actual resolved events. |

Store mutually exclusive outcomes as one value, rather than unrelated booleans that can all become true. Accepting a plan does not complete it: repair, rescue, and release actions validate prerequisites at the moment of execution. Reward completion once, while allowing repeatable aftermath conversations.

## 8. Delivery and validation

Build the first slice as a coherent set: Court of the First Cup, caravan enclosure, vessel dungeon, Nima and Tarek, Solari reactions, optional Samir scenes, and visible aftermath. Other regions then reuse the authoring pattern with different mechanics and beliefs.

An implementation is complete only when:

- Every required clue has a reachable source and the critical rule has a second way to learn it.
- The route works without Samir, without Tarek's testimony, and without high trust.
- Supporters, captives, deserters, and committed enemies behave according to their actual status.
- Negotiated and combat routes reach valid outcomes, including after a key antagonist dies.
- Site changes agree with saved outcomes after leaving, returning, and reloading.
- Repeat conversations neither duplicate rewards nor undo prior decisions.
- Evidence does not imply more than it shows; NPCs acknowledge contradictions and revise claims when warranted.
- Religious life remains visible outside hostile encounters, and local believers participate in resolving abuse.

The design goal is a connected experience: a custom learned over a shared meal helps the player understand a camp, a camp clue changes a dungeon conversation, and the dungeon's resolution changes what people do and say at home.
